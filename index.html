What is AI?

AI is a computer program you talk to in plain English. It answers like a well-read helper who never gets tired of questions.

Good at

Writing, explaining, planning and answering questions.

Bad at

It can be wrong and still sound sure. It only knows what you tell it about you.

Costs

Free to start. Paid plans are about $8 to $20 a month. You don't need one to begin.

Try it now

  1. Go to chatgpt.com, claude.ai or gemini.google.com. Any one is fine.
  2. Click in the box at the bottom.
  3. Type this, then press Enter:
I'm new to AI. In simple words, what are 5 useful things you could help me with this week?

Tap "Copy," then paste it into the AI. There are no wrong questions.

What you need (and what you don't)

The AI runs on the company's giant computers, not yours. Like electricity: the power plant is huge, but you just plug in a lamp.

You need

  • A phone, tablet or computer you already own. Anything from the last 5 years or so.
  • Your regular internet. Home Wi-Fi or your phone's data.
  • An email address, to make a free account.
  • Optional: earbuds or a speaker, if you'd like to talk to it.

You don't need

  • A new or powerful computer. The heavy work happens on their end.
  • Coding or programming. You type or talk in plain English.
  • A special network or server. Regular internet is plenty.
  • Complicated software. It's a website or a free app.
  • To pay. Start with the free version.

Myth or fact?

"AI is for tech people." Myth. If you can send a text message, you can use AI.

"I might break something." Myth. You can't. If an answer goes sideways, start a new chat.

"Building an app takes a programmer." Not anymore. "Vibe coding" means describing what you want in plain words, and the AI builds it.

"It's always listening." Myth. A chatbot only sees what you type or say while it's open. You can check its privacy settings any time.

"My old phone won't work." Usually it will. If the app won't install, use the website in your phone's browser instead.

Five safety rules before you start

  • Never type in your Social Security number, bank or card numbers, or passwords.
  • AI can be confidently wrong. Double-check anything about health, money or the law with a real person.
  • Use the official app or website. Search for the name yourself rather than clicking a link someone sent you.
  • Start with the free version. Pay only after it has helped you for a few weeks.
  • If a message, call or "AI" asks for money or gift cards in a hurry, stop. That is a scam pattern. The AARP Fraud Watch Network helpline (877-908-3360) is free to call.
